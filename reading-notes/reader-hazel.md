# Reader-Hazel: reading note

**Book:** The Restaurant of Many Orders — Miyazawa Kenji
**Original title:** 注文の多い料理店
**Language:** Japanese
**Source:** https://www.aozora.gr.jp/cards/000081/files/43754_17659.html
**Section read:** The whole story, from the two gentlemen lost in the mountains to their return to Tokyo; I lingered on the ending, from the dogs breaking through the door to the restaurant vanishing.

**Quote (verbatim):**
> 　室はけむりのように消え、二人は寒さにぶるぶるふるえて、草の中に立っていました。

**Translation:** The room vanished like smoke, and the two of them stood shivering with cold in the grass. (my translation)

**Thought:** Kenji builds the story as a loop. The line "風がどうと吹いてきて、草はざわざわ、木の葉はかさかさ、木はごとんごとんと鳴りました" (the wind gusts, the grass rustles, the leaves crackle, the trees creak) appears word for word just before the restaurant shows up and again right after it vanishes. Between those two refrains is a fox-spirit trap made of polite signs, each one a little more absurd ("rub the salt into your whole body"). The gentlemen keep reading the signs flatteringly and in their own favour until the last door. The punishment that stays with me is small and lasting: their faces, crumpled "like wastepaper" from fear, never recover, not even after a hot bath in Tokyo. The mountain lets them go but keeps a mark on them. It is also a sharp joke about a club like this one, where you walk in through a series of doors that say "please come in, don't hold back."
