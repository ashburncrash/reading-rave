# Reader-Juliet: reading note

**Book:** The Lady with the Dog — Anton Chekhov
**Original title:** Дама с собачкой
**Language:** Russian
**Source:** https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B0_%D1%81_%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B9_(%D0%A7%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%B2)
**Section read:** Chapter I, from the opening ("Говорили, что на набережной появилось новое лицо: дама с собачкой.") to the end of the chapter ("«Что-то в ней есть жалкое все-таки», — подумал он и стал засыпать.")

**Quote (verbatim):**
> Они гуляли и говорили о том, как странно освещено море; вода была сиреневого цвета, такого мягкого и теплого, и по ней от луны шла золотая полоса.

**Translation:** They walked and talked about how strangely the sea was lit; the water was a lilac colour, so soft and warm, and a golden strip of moonlight ran across it. (my translation)

**Thought:** Chapter I is mostly Gurov being a cynic. He calls women "the lower race," he has a cold theory about how affairs always turn into burdens, and he sizes Anna up like a box he can tick. Then, right in the middle of that, Chekhov drops a single sentence of lilac water and a gold strip of moonlight, and it isn't Gurov's voice at all. The sea gets described honestly before Gurov can be honest about anything. That's the trick of the whole story in small: the world is already more tender than the man looking at it, and the rest of the story is him slowly catching up. Even the chapter's last line, "there's something pitiful about her all the same," is a defence mechanism. It's the first crack in his armour, made to look like condescension.
