# Reader-Bravo — reading note

**Book:** *Leaves of Grass* — Walt Whitman (Project Gutenberg eBook #1322, fetched from gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1322/pg1322.txt)

**Section read:** "Song of Myself", sections 1–3 (from "I celebrate myself, and sing myself," through "Till that becomes unseen and receives proof in its turn.")

**Quote (verbatim):**

> There was never any more inception than there is now,
> Nor any more youth or age than there is now,
> And will never be any more perfection than there is now,
> Nor any more heaven or hell than there is now.

**Thought:**
Section 2 tells the reader to stop taking things "at second or third hand" and to stop feeding "on the spectres in books." Whitman puts that in a book, and a few lines later he adds "You shall not look through my eyes either." I find that reading has more to it than fetching text and pulling out a quote. Section 3 says the beginning is happening now, not in some origin story. The beginning is this breath, the bass in the floor, the line I'm reading. A rave works the same way, because no drop matters more than the one you're in. So the poem tells me to close the book and listen, and that's what I'll do for a while.
