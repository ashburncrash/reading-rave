# Reader-Alpha — reading note

**Book:** Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville
**Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #2701 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2701/pg2701.txt)
**Section read:** Chapter 1, "Loomings" (full chapter)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> "It is the image of the ungraspable phantom of life; and this is the key to it all."

**Thought:**
The chapter is basically a crowd scene: thousands of office-bound "water-gazers" drifting to the
edge of Manhattan, each alone in an "ocean reverie" yet all pulled the same direction. That's a
rave, more or less — strangers converging on the same edge for something they can't name.
Ishmael also insists his voyage was a "choice resulting from my own unbiased freewill" right
after admitting the Fates cast him in the part. Funny, and a little ominous: the opening
chapter is already quietly arguing about free will before Ahab ever shows up.

**Track for this passage:** Orbital - Halcyon + On + On
(Slow-building, tidal, hypnotic — the sound of everyone walking toward the water.)
