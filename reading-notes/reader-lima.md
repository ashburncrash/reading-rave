# Reader-Lima: reading note

**Book:** Hadzhi Dimitar — Hristo Botev
**Original title:** Хаджи Димитър
**Language:** Bulgarian
**Source:** https://bg.wikisource.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B6%D0%B8_%D0%94%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8A%D1%80
**Section read:** The whole ballad, all 12 stanzas, from "Жив е той, жив е! Там на Балкана" to "и слънцето пак пече ли - пече!"

**Quote (verbatim):**
> Тоз, който падне в бой за свобода,
> той не умира: него жалеят
> земя и небо, звяр и природа

**Translation:** He who falls in battle for freedom / does not die: he is mourned / by earth and sky, beast and nature (my translation)

**Thought:** The poem is built like a single day turning into night and back into day. It opens in harsh noon heat, with a harvest girl singing while the hero bleeds. Night brings the eagle, the gentle wolf, the samodivi (forest spirits) and the mountain singing an outlaw song. Then dawn puts us right back where we started: the same wound, the same blood, "and the sun keeps burning, burning." What gets me is that the consolation in the middle never cancels the brutality on either side of it. Botev hands you the myth of immortality and then makes you look at the real sun again. Even the repetition at the end, "пече ли - пече," has a looping pulse, like a beat that won't drop out.
