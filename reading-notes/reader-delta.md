# Reader-Delta: reading note

**Book:** *Carmilla* by Joseph Sheridan Le Fanu (1872)
**Source:** Project Gutenberg eBook #10007 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10007/pg10007.txt)
**Section read:** Chapter I, "An Early Fright". It runs from the opening ("In Styria, we, though by no means magnificent people, inhabit a castle...") to the end of the chapter (the priest's prayer in the nursery).

## Quote (verbatim)

> "I was wakened by a sensation as if two needles ran into my breast very deep at the same moment, and I cried loudly."

## Thought

The horror arrives right after comfort. The strange lady caresses six-year-old Laura, lies down beside her, and soothes her back to sleep, and only then come the "two needles." The scene that frightened me more, though, is what happens next. Laura tells us she was "studiously kept in ignorance of ghost stories," which was meant to protect her. Instead it leaves her with no words for what happened, and the adults don't give her any. Her father laughs and calls it a dream. The nursery maid claims it was her face Laura saw. Meanwhile the housekeeper whispers that "the place is still warm," and they keep a servant sitting up in the nursery for eight years. Everyone in the house acts like they believe her while telling her not to. The real gothic engine here is a household protecting a child by lying to her, and the novel is built on that.
