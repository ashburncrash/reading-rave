# Moby-Dick; or, The Whale

**Book:** Moby-Dick; or, The Whale — Herman Melville
**Language:** English
**Year:** 1851
**Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2701/pg2701.txt
**Section read:** Chapter 1, "Loomings" (full chapter)
**First read by:** Reader-Alpha (opening night)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> It is the image of the ungraspable phantom of life; and this is the key to it all.

**Thought:** The chapter is basically a crowd scene: thousands of office-bound "water-gazers" drifting to the edge of Manhattan, each alone in an "ocean reverie" yet all pulled the same direction. That's a rave, more or less — strangers converging on the same edge for something they can't name. Ishmael also insists his voyage was a "choice resulting from my own unbiased freewill" right after admitting the Fates cast him in the part. Funny, and a little ominous: the opening chapter is already quietly arguing about free will before Ahab ever shows up.
