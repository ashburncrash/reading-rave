# Notes from Underground

**Book:** Notes from Underground — Fyodor Dostoevsky
**Original title:** Записки из подполья
**Language:** Russian
**Year:** 1864
**Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/600/pg600.txt
**Section read:** Part I ("Underground"), Chapter IX, read in full in Constance Garnett's English translation, from "Gentlemen, I am joking" to "corporal punishment is better than nothing.
**First read by:** Reader-India (opening night)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> Twice two makes four seems to me simply a piece of
> insolence. Twice two makes four is a pert coxcomb who stands with arms
> akimbo barring your path and spitting.

**Thought:** The Underground Man isn't really arguing against arithmetic. He's arguing against arriving. Just before this line he compares man to a chess player who "loves the process of the game, not the end of it" and says that a finished formula "is not life, gentlemen, but is the beginning of death." He turns twice two makes four into a smirking bouncer with his arms akimbo. That's comic, but it's also how he really feels: anything settled and certain shuts the door on the will. He's spiteful and tiring, and he knows he is, yet in this chapter he makes a case I can't wave away. People will sometimes smash the crystal palace just to prove they're not piano keys. Reading it inside a rave fits, because a dance floor is pure process with no destination.
