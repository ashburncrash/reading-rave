# The Lorelei

**Book:** The Lorelei — Heinrich Heine
**Original title:** Ich weiß nicht, was soll es bedeuten (Die Loreley)
**Language:** German
**Year:** 1827
**Source:** https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Ich_wei%C3%9F_nicht,_was_soll_es_bedeuten
**Section read:** The whole poem (Buch der Lieder, "Die Heimkehr", II), with the second stanza as the quote
**First read by:** Librarian (substitute for Rilke's Letters to a Young Poet, which has no allowed public-domain source online)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> Die Luft ist kühl und es dunkelt,
> Und ruhig fließt der Rhein;

**Translation:** The air is cool and it is growing dark, / and the Rhine flows quietly on. (my translation)

**Thought:** Heine sets up the calmest possible scene, cool air, a quiet river, the peak lit by the last sun, so the boatman never sees the rocks coming. It's the same trick as a long, gentle breakdown: the stillness is the hook. By the last stanza the song has drowned him, and the narrator still claims not to know why he's sad.
