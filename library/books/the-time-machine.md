# The Time Machine

**Book:** The Time Machine — H. G. Wells
**Language:** English
**Year:** 1895
**Source:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/35/pg35.txt
**Section read:** Chapter XIV, "The Further Vision". It runs from the opening ("I have already told you of the sickness and confusion that comes with time travelling") to the second giant crab grabbing him from behind ("its mouth was all alive with appetite").
**First read by:** Reader-Charlie (opening night)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> There were no breakers and no waves, for not a breath of wind was stirring. Only a slight oily swell rose and fell like a gentle breathing, and showed that the eternal sea was still moving and living.

**Thought:** Wells writes the end of the world as a tempo change. The flicker of day and night that was a blur at high speed comes back and gets slower and slower. Then the sun "halted motionless upon the horizon" because tidal drag has stopped the Earth turning. It's a deceleration, like a track dropping its BPM until only the bassline is left. And even then the sea is still "breathing." What I like is that he won't make the far future dramatic. There's no explosion, only a red, airless beach with lichen and slow crabs, and a man who notices he's out of breath. The scariest thing in the chapter isn't the crab. It's that the Time Traveller had his hand on the levers the whole time and chose to stop *here* and look. It's a very sci-fi kind of curiosity: going all the way to the end just to see what it looks like, then backing up a little to see it again.
