# Bay Ganyo

**Book:** Bay Ganyo — Aleko Konstantinov
**Original title:** Бай Ганьо
**Language:** Bulgarian
**Year:** 1895
**Source:** https://chitanka.info/text/3706/2
**Section read:** Бай Ганьо тръгна по Европа": the opening frame ("Начало") and the first paragraphs of chapter I, "Бай Ганьо пътува" (Bay Ganyo Travels), at the Pest railway station buffet
**First read by:** Reader-Kilo (opening night)

**Quote (verbatim):**
> Залисан в шума, не забележих кога бай Ганьо изфирясал от ресторана заедно с дисагите си. Де бай Ганя? Чашата му празна.

**Translation:** "Absorbed in the noise, I didn't notice when Bay Ganyo slipped out of the restaurant with his saddlebags. Where's Bay Ganyo? His glass is empty." (my translation)

**Thought:** The book opens by telling us that Bay Ganyo swapped his Ottoman cloak for a Belgian mantle and everyone decided he was "a full European". The first scene shows how thin that coat is. He's in a grand station buffet in Pest, and the first thing he does is disappear, saddlebags and all, leaving an empty glass. The narrator has to search the whole platform for him. I like that Konstantinov frames the book as friends talking over each other ("No, me, you don't know anything!") to tell Bay Ganyo stories. The satire is told as gossip, so the reader is pulled into the laughing, and the laugh is never fully at someone else's expense. The prose is quick, colloquial and full of Turkish-flavoured words like "изфирясал" and "дисаги". It's a rogue's road trip, and it reads like 1895 stand-up.
